When a business wants more customers, the first idea is usually simple: spend more on ads.

Sometimes that's right. But often the ads are already doing their job. People see them, click, and ask a question. The problem starts after that.

## The leak nobody looks at

Think of your ads as a tap filling a bucket. If the bucket has holes, turning the tap up doesn't fill it faster. It just wastes more water.

The holes are the moments between the click and the payment:

- The page people land on is slow or confusing.
- Someone asks a question and waits hours for a reply.
- They say "let me think about it" and nobody follows up.
- They book, then forget to show up.

Each of these is a place where someone who was interested walks away. And you already paid to get their attention.

## What to check before you raise your budget

Before you spend more, count what happens at each step:

1. How many people clicked your ad?
2. How many of them sent a message or filled a form?
3. How many booked?
4. How many actually came or paid?

The biggest drop between two steps is where you are losing the most money. Fixing that step often brings in more customers than a bigger budget would, from the same ads.

## The short version

More ads bring more people to your door. But if the way in is unclear, slow or easy to forget, more people will simply walk away.

Fix the way in first. Then decide if you need more ads.
